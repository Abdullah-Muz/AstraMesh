## Findings so far (Priority A; assistant draft)

1. **Prior art (D01, D02):** NASA's Starling 1.0 already flew four CubeSats coordinating dual-band GPS ionospheric observations over crosslinks, with distributed onboard planning and peer takeover. Any AstraMesh contribution must lie elsewhere: health beliefs vs communication loss, partition reconciliation, protection modes, quantified crosslink constraints, or reproducible baselines (spec §9.2 G1–G4), and still needs checking against the technical DSA papers.
2. **Communication realism is the common gap.** P03, P19 and P22 all assume an idealised network: no loss or latency (P03, P19) or instant links (P22). P08's theory covers connectivity and uniform delay but not loss or partitions. AstraMesh's network model (DL-040) already goes further, so baselines borrowed from these papers must be run under it as published *and* labelled where adapted.
3. **B3 (CBBA):** P06 is now read, and its reimplementation specification is in [`literature/P06.md`](literature/P06.md) §3. CBBA's guarantees hold only for DMG scores, synchronous conflict resolution and a static connected network, with no loss model. B3 will therefore run synchronous rounds over AstraMesh's network, record which assumptions are violated, and test the observation score for DMG. Choices the paper leaves open (tie-breaking, stop rule, message order within a round, timestamp handling) will be fixed and documented in a decision-log entry. P03 is the satellite-adapted variant to compare against.
4. **Post-loss redistribution (P09)** is weak evidence: an analogy with no network and one run. It is still a citable rule-based precedent and a cheap comparator for B4.
5. **G3 (Phase 5, D10, R01, R02; assistant draft):** Starling's flight results (D10) show distributed science planning, not protection: no space-weather response, protection modes or health beliefs are described. R01 shows with real data that storm impact on a LEO constellation differs between orbital planes and satellites and from day to day, and calls for storm-aware autonomous modes and collective decisions without proposing one. R02 frames a protection decision as false go against false no-go, for a single asset with persistence only. R04 finds that pBFT consensus among intermittently linked satellites completed within 3.3 h in only half of its cases, so a G3 rule should not wait for fleet-wide agreement before a spacecraft protects itself. R05 (operator survey, 55 responses) finds that satellite operators mostly kept operating through the 2024 storms, rarely used safe mode because lost service outweighed the risk, managed the fleet "as a whole", and asked for localized warnings and quantitative cost-benefit frameworks. R03 gives the evaluation tool: cost-loss forecast value across the whole cost/loss range, which suits AstraMesh because the loss from an unprotected hazard is synthetic. P01S (read 2026-11-10) confirms Finding 1. D11 (DSA 1.5, 2026) adds reactive onboard rules with dwell times for the crosslink radios and states that the swarm plans and executes without enforced consensus, "prioritiz[ing] agility of response over consistency"; it has no space-weather or protection logic. Together they support G3 as a gap; the closest-work search found no G3 method among 8 items read in full; R07 (low–medium) is still unread; R06 was read for increment 3.
