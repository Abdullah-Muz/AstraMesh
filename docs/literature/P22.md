## 2. Analysis columns

**problem**
- The paper asks how to tell two kinds of fault apart in a leader–follower swarm. A *global* fault is a leader reference or navigation bias that all followers see. A *local* fault is one follower's actuator or sensor fault (§1, problem statement on p.3).
- It also targets "fault absorption", where an online-learning detector adapts to a persistent fault and stops flagging it (§2.2 after Eq. 4; §2.3.1).
- Two goals are stated: "Isolation Accuracy" and "Model Integrity" (p.3).

**agent_info**
- One leader and N followers. The simulation uses 1 leader and 3 followers (§3.1, Fig. 1).
- Each link or follower keeps an online Bayesian Ridge Regression model of its healthy residual baseline. It computes a Wasserstein anomaly score S_i(t) from the difference between the physics-predicted and measured relative position (Eqs. 3–5; §2.2).
- Agents share scalar anomaly scores. The paper says only the scalar Global Consensus Score has to be broadcast (§3.2, p.14).
- There is no belief/health-state machine. The outputs are only "local follower fault", "global leader fault", or no fault (§2.3.3). Fig. 7A's heatmap uses three colours (Healthy / Anomalous / Faulty): the Leader row is Faulty for steps 200–400, all three followers are Anomalous for 200–400, and Follower 1 is Faulty for about 600–800. How these labels are computed is not described.

**networking_assumptions**
- Star topology: "All followers communicate directly with the leader" over L links (§3.1; §2.3.2). Fig. 1A confirms this: three dashed links, leader to each follower, no follower–follower links.
- §3.4 item 4 states the consensus logic "assumes instantaneous availability of data from all followers"; asynchronous operation is left to future work.
- No link model, latency, loss or bandwidth model is described. The text claims ">85% detection performance even at 60% connectivity loss" and attributes it to Fig. 9A (p.14), but the rendered **Fig. 9A is "Theoretical Scalability Analysis"** (precision and recall vs swarm size). **No figure in the paper shows connectivity loss.**
- Fig. 7C ("Communication Link Reliability") drops to 0 for all three links during steps 200–400 and for L-F1 only during about 600–800, and sits near 0.95 otherwise. This tracks the fault windows exactly, so it appears to be derived from the fault labels rather than from any communication model; the method is not stated.

**physics**
- Linear Hill–Clohessy–Wiltshire (HCW) relative dynamics written as a continuous LTI state-space model (Eqs. 1–2). The orbital rate n is 0.0011 rad/s. (The text then says one orbit takes "about 5.712 s (roughly 95 min)"; 2π/n is about 5712 s.)
- The scenario is a 500 km LEO, tetrahedron formation with 100 m separation, and Gaussian sensor noise (§3.1). Fig. 1A shows the leader near the origin and followers at roughly ±100 m.
- The text contradicts itself here:
  - §3.1 says "full orbital propagation".
  - §3.3 says deterministic dynamics (Eq. 1) were used "to intentionally remove external environmental perturbations".
  - J2 and drag appear as d in Eq. 1, but the limitations section says they are not modelled (§3.4).
- There is no attitude, power, thermal or communication physics.

**fault_model**
- Two step/bias faults, injected one at a time in a 1000-step run (§3.1, Fig. 1B):
  - **Global:** a "sudden GPS broadcast bias of 15 m" injected into the Leader for t ∈ [200,400].
  - **Local:** a "physical position bias of 12 m" injected into Follower 1 for t ∈ [600,800].
- Fig. 1B confirms the timing (fault status 1 for the Leader over 200–400 and Follower 1 over 600–800).
- Fault magnitudes and noise levels are stated inconsistently:
  - The Fig. 1 description gives the leader fault as a 20 m bias on X, Y and Z, and the Follower 1 fault as a 15 m bias on X only (p.8). Fig. 1A draws only short, unlabelled fault-vector arrows at the Leader and Follower 1, so the figure does not settle the magnitudes.
  - Fig. 3A partly supports §3.1 for the local fault: standard MGOTL's model mean converges to a plateau of about 12 m during 600–800 (it goes off the 16 m axis during the leader fault, so that magnitude cannot be read).
  - Fig. 2's residuals are "scaled for visualization clarity" (p.9): during 200–400, L-F1 is about 76 m and L-F2/L-F3 about 27 m; during 600–800, only L-F1 rises, to about 32 m. These do not map onto either stated magnitude.
  - Noise is given as σ_noise ≈ 5 cm in §3.1, σ = 0.005 m (5 mm) in §2.3.4, and σ = 0.05 (no unit) in §3.3.
- Faults are injected directly as biases into the measurement or broadcast stream. There are no crash, silence, intermittent or Byzantine faults in the described simulation.
- Simultaneous leader and follower faults are explicitly excluded by assumption (§3.4 item 3).
- The text attributes ">88%" detection under "multiple fault" and "cascade" scenarios to Fig. 9B, but the rendered **Fig. 9B is "Noise Robustness Analysis"**; no figure reports detection rates for multiple or cascade faults. The only figure that names such scenarios is Fig. 8D (detection latency bars for "Single Fault", "Multiple Faults", "Cascade Faults", "Intermittent Faults"). None of these scenarios is defined anywhere in the text.

**safety_model**
- Not stated in the text. There are no protection or safe modes, no recovery actions, and no constraint enforcement.
- The conclusions frame IF-CAD as a diagnostic "front-end" for fault-tolerant control (FTC) that is not implemented (§4, p.17).
- The "stability" analysis covers only the learner's parameters, not vehicle safety (§2.3.5).

**method**
- **Residual.** The residual is r_i = |P_measured − P_target|, where P_target(k) = A·X(k−1) + B·U(k−1) (Eq. 3). Note that A is used here as the continuous-time matrix of Eq. 2.
- **Learner and anomaly score.** An MGOTL-style online model, using Bayesian Ridge Regression, gives a Wasserstein-p anomaly score (Eqs. 4–5) with an adaptive-threshold ratio test N_s/N_ω > ε (Eq. 6).
- **Smart-Freeze.** The baseline is updated as an exponential moving average, μ_t = (1−α)μ_{t−1} + α·r_t, with α = 0.05, only while S_i < τ. Otherwise μ is held (Eqs. 7, 12–13; Eq. 12 writes the same switch on W ≤ τ).
- **Global Consensus Score.** G(t) is the arithmetic mean of the link scores (Eq. 8, rendered as (1/L)ΣS_i). Fig. 4's description instead calls it the "geometric mean".
- **Isolation ratio.** R_i = S_i/(G+ε), ε = 10⁻⁶ (Eq. 9). Fig. 4's description instead uses the ratio of the L-F1 score to the max of the L-F2 and L-F3 scores, and Eq. 14 divides by (1/N)ΣW_j (N, not L).
- **Decision gate (§2.3.3).** (The text says it uses "Equations (2) and (3)", which are the dynamics and residual; Eqs. 8–9 are meant.)
  - If R_i > Γ and S_i > τ, the fault is local to follower i.
  - Else, if G > τ, the fault is global (leader).
- **Thresholds.** τ = 10 (about the 99th percentile of the noise residual) and Γ = 5 (a "knee point" from a sensitivity analysis that is not shown) (§2.3.4). Fig. 3C's description instead cites a freeze threshold of 3.0; the rendered Fig. 3C plots a normalised score and draws no threshold line, so the figure does not resolve this. Fig. 3B does draw a "Detection Threshold" dashed line at about 10.
- **Fig. 4 (consensus vs isolation).** The Global Consensus Metric sits at about 850 during 200–400 and near 0 elsewhere; the F1 Isolation Ratio is noisy during 600–800 (mostly up to about 1000, one spike above 3000) and near 0 during the leader fault. The τ = 10 and Γ = 5 thresholds are not distinguishable at this scale.
- **Stability argument.** The "Lyapunov/ISS" argument is informal: with α = 0 the parameters are constant (§2.3.5).

**baselines**
- One baseline: standard MGOTL (Xia & Meng 2025, ref. [25]) applied per link without freeze or isolation logic (§3.2–3.3).
- In Results, MGOTL is miscited as ref. [15], which is a GNSS paper (pp.8, 12, 14).
- There is no comparison with Kalman, parity-space or other distributed FDI methods.

**metrics**
- Confusion-matrix TP/FP/FN/TN, precision, recall and F1 per fault class (Tables 1–2).
- From figures (all checked visually; see the Visual check section for values): detection timelines (Fig. 5), ROC, precision–recall and threshold-sensitivity F1 (Fig. 6), a health dashboard with consensus health, "link reliability", isolation confidence, resilience and degradation (Fig. 7), capability bar chart, compute time, FPR over time, detection latency and cumulative energy (Fig. 8), and swarm-size, noise, compute, communication-overhead, FPR and memory curves (Fig. 9).

**scale**
- 4 spacecraft (1 leader, 3 followers), 1000 time steps, two fault episodes of 200 steps each (§3.1).
- Tables 1–2 are called "1000-step Monte Carlo" results (captions: "(1000 steps)"), but the number of runs and the seeds are not stated in the text or in the tables. Each table row sums to 1000 steps, which reads like a single run.
- Scalability to "dozens or hundreds" of agents is claimed (O(1) per node) but not demonstrated (p.14). Figs. 8B, 9C, 9D and 9F plot compute, communication overhead and memory against 3–15 or 3–20 nodes as straight lines, with IF-CAD above MGOTL in each; no method or units are given. Fig. 9A ("Theoretical Scalability Analysis") shows precision falling from 1.0 at 3 satellites to about 0.76 at 15, and recall to about 0.88, which cuts against the scalability claim.

**key_limitations**
- The authors themselves name four (§3.4; §4): linearised HCW with a circular orbit, Gaussian noise, no simultaneous leader and follower faults, and instantaneous communication.
- Further limitations I observed:
  - Results come from one deterministic scenario. Table 1 shows perfect 199/0/0/801 for IF-CAD, with identical rows for Leader and F1.
  - The parameters (fault magnitudes, σ, thresholds, the definitions of G and R) are internally inconsistent, as listed above. The figures do not resolve these, except that Fig. 3A is consistent with a 12 m local fault.
  - Several text-to-figure references are wrong: the connectivity-loss and multiple/cascade claims cite Fig. 9A/9B, which show other things; noise robustness is cited as Fig. 9E but is Fig. 9B; ROC is cited as "Figure 5A" (it is 6A); PR/threshold as "Figures 5C and 6B" (6B, 6C); ground truth as "Figure 4" (5A); the §3.4 "phase space (Figure 8)" does not exist in Fig. 8.
  - Many figure-based claims have no described method: energy, memory, compute, latency, link reliability, and "capability" scores. Where figures exist, they show values without method or units (see Visual check). The "60% connectivity loss" claim has no figure at all.
  - The text asserts that MGOTL ends up "closer to the random classifier line". The rendered Fig. 6A actually shows MGOTL's ROC curve **below** the random diagonal (worse than chance), while IF-CAD is drawn at TPR = 1 for every FPR.
  - Table 2's IF-CAD rows are not internally consistent: TP 196, FP 5, FN 3 give recall ≈ 0.985 and precision ≈ 0.975, but the table prints precision 0.985 and recall 1.0. Its MGOTL recall (2/199 ≈ 0.010) is printed as 0.005.
  - The "guarantees" in the text are rhetorical rather than proven.
- **Demonstrated** vs **claimed:**
  - Demonstrated, in one nominal scenario: freezing the per-link adaptation keeps the anomaly score high during a persistent step bias (Fig. 3), and the ratio gate separates a common-mode leader bias from a single-follower bias (Figs. 4–5). Fig. 5B shows MGOTL firing only at fault start and end, which matches Table 1's TP = 1, FP = 1.
  - Claimed only (figures without method, or no figure): robustness to connectivity loss, multi-fault and cascade handling, scalability, energy and memory cost.

**AstraMesh_overlap** (cautious)
- **Common-mode attribution.** The common-mode vs single-node idea maps loosely onto AstraMesh's distributed fault attribution. A deviation everyone sees points to a shared or reference cause, while a deviation one agent shows points to that agent.
- **Freeze-on-anomaly.** Not adapting baselines while an anomaly is active is a cheap, replicable guard. AstraMesh could use it in any adaptive health-belief threshold so that DEGRADED behaviour is not learned as normal.
- **Communication loss vs failure: no help.**
  - The paper does **not** separate communication loss from failure. It assumes instantaneous, complete data and has no silence or crash fault class. Fig. 7C's "link reliability" drops to zero during sensor/broadcast-bias faults, i.e. it conflates fault presence with link state.
  - It only asserts that the Γ buffer resists "transient communication drops" (§2.3.5 item 3).
  - It has no counterpart to AstraMesh's COMMUNICATION_LOST, SUSPECTED_UNAVAILABLE, CONFIRMED_FAILED, RECOVERING or UNKNOWN distinctions. It also has no belief-vs-truth separation beyond comparing detections with ground truth.
- **Topology mismatch.** The paper's topology is centralised: a leader star with a broadcast score. AstraMesh is a peer crosslink mesh.
- **Use in AstraMesh.** Cite it as a related heuristic for attributing local vs shared faults. Do not cite it as validated evidence.

## 3. replication_priority

**low.** The mechanism is simple: an EMA freeze plus a mean-ratio gate. The evidence is a single 4-satellite deterministic scenario with internally inconsistent parameters, and the robustness claims either lack a figure or cite the wrong one. The paper also does not address communication loss vs failure, which is AstraMesh's main concern. A small ablation inside AstraMesh would be more informative than replicating the paper.
