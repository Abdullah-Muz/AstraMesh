## 2. Analysis columns

**problem:** DSA 1.5 on Starling's extended mission: better onboard TEC (absolute TEC from carrier-smoothed pseudoranges with bias corrections), autonomy that commands hardware (the crosslink radios), and robust large-file transfer across the swarm (abstract).

**agent_info:** as DSA 1.0 (P01S): each spacecraft combines its own TEC with peers' and plans for the swarm at 1 Hz. New: a PLEXIL plan executive (cFS application) for reactive, rule-based commanding (pp. 3–5).

**networking_assumptions:** the swarm plans and executes **without** enforced consensus: "If any spacecraft had different information for any reason, the swarm would still plan and execute with the lack of consensus. This approach prioritizes agility of response over consistency of the swarm's behavior" (p. 2). Large files (10 MB) were transferred across network interruptions of up to 60 min (abstract).

**physics:** none modelled; region rules use latitude bands (±80°, ±15°) (pp. 4–5).

**fault_model:** none studied. Operational context: one spacecraft's ground receive radio had failed (uplink limited to 200 kB/day); only three spacecraft took part (pp. 2, 7).

**safety_model / mode logic:** PLEXIL rules turn the crosslink radios on in regions of interest or when a TEC feature is detected, and off again, with minimum dwell times: on only if off for at least 30 min; off after 30 min on, or after 10 min on with no feature (pp. 5–6, Fig. 4). A 1 h radio duty cycle was chosen "to preserve battery … while avoiding safe mode" (p. 5). A rule commanding radios from **battery voltage** (BattVoltTest) failed both times it flew: an unrelated crash on 22 Aug, incorrect voltage telemetry on 5 Sep (Table 1).

**baselines / metrics:** none quantitative for autonomy; Table 1 lists each flight scenario's outcome (success, failure, partial success).

**key_limitations:** 3 spacecraft; no quantitative results on the radio rules' effect; TEC validation against COSMIC-2 is planned, not done (p. 3).

**AstraMesh_overlap:**
- Flight evidence for a design choice G3 needs: act on local information without enforced consensus, accepting inconsistency between spacecraft for faster response. It agrees with P01S (frequent loss of consensus) and R04 (consensus can take hours).
- Its dwell-time radio rules are hysteresis-style mode logic, applied to power and science, not hazard protection. No space weather, radiation or protection modes appear, so G3 is still not covered by the Starling/DSA line of work.
