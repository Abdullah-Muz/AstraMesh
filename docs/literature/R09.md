## 2. Analysis columns

**problem (of the section read):** how the geomagnetic field sets the minimum rigidity a charged particle needs to reach a location, and practical approximations for it.

**content:**
- Cutoffs depend on geomagnetic latitude, altitude and arrival direction; between the upper and lower cutoff lies a chaotic penumbra, and the *effective* cutoff corrects for transmission through it (p. 6-9, 6-10).
- Trajectory-derived effective vertical cutoffs for Epoch 1980 on a 5° × 15° world grid (Table 6-3, pp. 6-10 to 6-11) and as a contour map (Figure 6-13).
- **Störmer:** P_c = M cos⁴λ / (R² [1 + (1 − sin ε sin φ cos³λ)^{1/2}]²) (eq. 6.16; λ geomagnetic latitude, ε zenith angle, φ azimuth from magnetic north); normalised to Earth's dipole moment with R in Earth radii the constant is 59.6, and the **vertical** cutoff is **P_c = 14.9 cos⁴λ / R² GV** (eq. 6.17, p. 6-12).
- **L-normalised form:** the trajectory-derived world grid normalised to McIlwain's L gives **P_c = 16.0 L⁻² GV** (eq. 6.18). It holds "where the geomagnetic field retains its basic dipolar structure", with "a practical limit … within the domain of the L = 4 shell. At higher L-shells increasing errors will be encountered due to the external current systems of the magnetosphere" (p. 6-12). A cutoff known at one altitude can be carried to another through the L⁻² dependence.
- **High latitudes:** where field lines are open the cutoff is essentially zero; between about 60° and 75° magnetic latitude there is a significant daily variation; magnetospheric models give only upper limits there, and the best average values come from polar-orbiting satellites (Fanselow and Stone 1972), shown in Figure 6-14 as invariant-latitude cutoffs against magnetic local time for 1.3–24.4 MeV protons. By eye, the 12.2 MeV cutoff lies at about 66°–70° invariant latitude over the day and the 24.4 MeV cutoff about 1°–2° lower (p. 6-12).

**key_limitations:** quiet-time, 1985 field models; Table 6-3 is for Epoch 1980 and the ground (20 km reference not used here); Figure 6-14 is read by eye from a scan; no storm-time cutoff changes in this section.
