## 2. Analysis columns

**problem:** measure where the geomagnetic cutoff for low-energy SEP protons and alpha particles lies during large SEP events, how fast it moves, how well geomagnetic indices predict it, and what that means for radiation exposure in the ISS orbit (abstract, §1).

**data and method:** SAMPEX (520 × 670 km, 82° inclination), six SEP periods from October 1992 to November 1998 (Figure 1). The cutoff is the invariant latitude (from IGRF 1990, cos²Λ = 1/L) at which a count rate falls to half its mean above 70°, measured to about 0.2° at each of the four polar-cap crossings per orbit (§2). Rates: PET 20–29 MeV and 29–64 MeV protons, MAST Z1 (~10 MeV protons) and mainly MAST Z2 (8–15 MeV/nucleon He, rigidity ~250–340 MV, "equivalent to ~30–60 MeV protons"; §2–3). Orbit-averaged cutoffs are compared with Dst and Kp (§3.1); individual crossings are corrected to Dst = 0 and fitted with an offset circle in invariant latitude against magnetic local time (§3.2).

**results:**
- The cutoff moves "often by ~5°–10° in less than a day" and tracks Dst closely, down to ≲ 1° and several-hour features (§3, Figure 3).
- Orbit-averaged cutoff against Dst: correlation 0.76 (Kp: −0.77), spread ~2°–3° at a given index value; "a change in Dst by 200 nT during a major geomagnetic storm moves the cutoff latitude by ~8°–10°" for the Z2 He (§3.1, Figures 5–6).
- The first-order Dst correction used for the local-time study is to subtract **Dst/19.11** (degrees, Dst in nT), i.e. 0.052° per nT (§3.2).
- At Dst = 0 the Z2 cutoffs fit an offset circle of radius **25.85 ± 0.04° (north) and 25.82 ± 0.04° (south)**, i.e. a cutoff near **64.2° invariant latitude**, with the centre offset 0.75–0.85° toward ~21–22 h MLT; scatter about the circle somewhat above 1° (§3.2, Figure 7). Residual local-time variability is ≲ 2° through the day (§5).
- Rigidity: in November 1997 the MAST ~10 MeV proton cutoffs are plotted with a −1° offset to overlay the He cutoffs (Figure 3), i.e. ~10 MeV protons are cut off about 1° poleward of Z2 He; an earlier SAMPEX study found ~3°–4° between ~300 MV and the 0 MV extrapolation (§3.2).
- Dst can lag the cutoff suppression by nearly half a day (day 306 of 1992) and is "an unreliable indicator of cutoff suppressions during the most critical periods"; Kp leads or lags at other times (§3.1, §5).
- Occasional single-crossing excursions of up to ~6°, mostly to higher (safer) latitudes, on the dayside (§3.2, Figure 8).
- ISS calculation (400 km, 51.6°, IGRF 1995, cutoff held for a day): a 5° suppression raises the average exposure time more than 2.5-fold, 15° more than 8-fold. No suppression as large as 15° was observed, but no period had Dst below about −150 nT; "Linear extrapolation (which may not be warranted) from Figure 5 suggests that a cutoff suppression of 15° might be associated with a geomagnetic storm with Dst around −300" (§4, Figure 10).

**key_limitations:** six SEP periods, Dst ≥ about −150 nT; mostly 8–15 MeV/n He, with ~10 MeV protons only in one period (Figure 3); invariant latitude from IGRF rather than the true (storm-distorted) field; local times unevenly sampled because SAMPEX's orbit precesses.

**AstraMesh_overlap (increment 3, DL-090):**
- Gives the storm-time slope directly: Δλ = Dst/19.11 °/nT. AstraMesh uses SYM-H, which the S11 series contains (DL-069), as the Dst proxy; R06 reports SYM-H and Dst correlating equally with the cutoff (0.78 each).
- Gives the quiet cutoff directly in invariant latitude: about 64.2° for ~300 MV He at Dst = 0, and about 1° poleward for ~10 MeV protons, so about **65.2°** for the ≥ 10 MeV hazard channel. This replaces the Störmer value as the quiet cutoff (R09 explains why: Störmer forms apply only within about L = 4, while 10 MeV protons are cut off near L ≈ 10).
- Does **not** support the May 2024 extreme: SYM-H reached −497 nT, and −354 nT during flux ≥ 100 pfu, while R08 observed only down to about −150 nT. Linear use there is an extrapolation the authors themselves doubt; the model needs a cap, and R08's own extrapolation (15° at about −300 nT) is a sourced choice for it.
- Supports a step access model: the cutoff is defined at half the polar rate and the transition is sharp (Figure 2: most of the rise within ~3°).
- The local-time offset (≲ 1°) and the Dst lag (up to half a day) are below or outside what a Dst-driven model can capture; both are limitations to state, not to model.
