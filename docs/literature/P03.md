## 2. Analysis columns

**problem:** Multi-satellite on-board observation planning (MSOOP), framed as a variant of multi-agent task allocation (MATAP). Satellites assign observation tasks (targets) to observation-availability windows ("masks") so as to maximise the total time-discounted profit. The constraints are storage (3), energy (4), at most one satellite per task ("consensus", 5), execution inside a mask (6), no overlap with attitude-manoeuvre time between observations (7), observation duration (8), and non-preemption (9) (§2.2). Constraints (3)–(5) and (7) are called "coupled" (§2.2). As rendered, Eq. (9) is only the binary domain x_ijk ∈ {0,1}; the prose calls it non-preemption, which is otherwise implied by (8). The equation forms are transcribed under **method**.

**agent_info:**
- **What each satellite stores:** its bundle, a winning-bid list over all tasks, a winning-satellite list over all tasks, a vector of timestamps for the last information from every other satellite, and per-mask enable flags (§3.1, "Algorithm-related variables").
- **What it sends:** only the winning-bid list, the winning-satellite list and the timestamp vector, sent to its current neighbours (§3.1, steps 2–3; Algorithm 1).
- **Initial values (Algorithm 1):** T = 0, b_i = ∅, y_ij = 0 and z_ij = −1 for all tasks, s_ii' = 0 for all satellites, conf_i = 0. Bundle construction and sending run only while conf_i = 0. conf_i is set by comparing y and z with the one neighbour just heard from (Algorithm 3).
- **What is known in advance:** the ground supplies orbit prediction and communication-availability windows. Tasks are broadcast to all satellites before planning. Each satellite computes its own observation windows (§3.1).
- **Not covered:** the paper states no sharing of satellite health or state beyond bids.

**networking_assumptions:**
- **Topology:** links are bidirectional. Intra-plane ISLs are always available and form a closed ring per plane. An inter-plane ISL is available "whenever the line-of-sight vector between two satellites passes above the surface of the earth" (§2.3, Fig. 2). A link exists at time t if t falls inside some communication availability mask [st^cam_i'il, et^cam_i'il] (§2.3).
- **Bandwidth:** planning messages are small, so "no strict demand for the bandwidth" (§2.3).
- **Latency and loss:** the Introduction mentions them as a communication cost, but they are not modelled in the method or the experiments ("not stated in the text" beyond §1).
- **Synchronisation:** satellite clocks are assumed to be synchronised with the ground (§3.1).
- **Convergence:** relies on CBBA's result that the union of time-varying topologies is connected. The authors assert this holds because no node is isolated (§3.1, §3.3).
- **Termination:** there is no detection of global termination. Each node runs until the earliest task start time or a preset runtime threshold (§3.1).

**physics:**
- **Constellation and simulator:** homogeneous Walker-δ constellations of identical optical satellites, 600 km altitude, 60° inclination (30/3/1, 60/3/1, 90/3/1). Observation and communication windows were computed with AGI STK (§2.1, §4).
- **Agility:** both non-agile (roll-only) and agile (three-axis) satellites are described. For agile satellites, execution times are set heuristically ("forward arrangement and central arrangement") (§2.2). The text does not state which agility model the experiments use, and gives no attitude-manoeuvre time values.
- **Energy:** no energy model is given. The text gives no energy parameter values (§4 lists only storage).

**fault_model:** The text gives no fault model. Robustness to loss of a central node is used only as an argument for decentralisation (§1). The only disturbance tested is dynamic task arrival: 100 tasks injected at iteration 30 (§4, Table 4, Fig. 4).

**safety_model:** The text gives no safety model, protection modes or health beliefs. Safety appears only as hard resource and timing constraints in the formulation (§2.2).

**method:** ICBBA is CBBA (Choi et al. 2009) with three changes (§1, §3):

- **(a) Constraint check in bundle construction.**
  - Each satellite greedily adds the task with the largest DMG that beats the currently known winning bid (Eq. 14).
  - The DMG of a task is computed from its earliest still-enabled window, with start time equal to the window start. This is a forward-arrangement rule (Eqs. 11–13).
  - After each addition, every window of every unassigned task is checked against storage, energy and timing constraints (3), (4), (6)–(8). Conflicting windows are disabled with a flag (§3.2, step 4; Algorithm 2).
- **(b) Mask recovery in the consensus check.**
  - This step otherwise follows standard CBBA. It applies the CBBA receiver action-rule table (update / reset / leave, Table 1) and the timestamp update (Eqs. 19–20).
  - The satellite then releases the earliest bundle task it no longer wins, plus all tasks added after it (Eqs. 21–24).
  - Finally, it re-enables windows that had been disabled only because of the released tasks (§3.3, step 4; Algorithm 3).
- **(c) Scene-specific bid functions** (§4):
  - DMG(P): discounted profit (Eq. 11).
  - DMG(P/S): profit per unit of estimated storage (Eq. 25), used for scattered "global" targets.
  - DMG(P-L): profit minus "Loss", the expected profit of windows that conflict with the chosen window under the timing constraints (Eq. 26). Used for dense "regional" targets, with storage and energy switched off.

The text does not show that these bid functions keep the diminishing-marginal-gain property that CBBA's convergence proof needs. The convergence claim relies on the original CBBA proof (§3.1). Reviewer's inference, not stated in the paper: DMG(P) depends on the bundle only through the mask flags, and adding tasks can only disable masks, so DMG(P) cannot rise as the bundle grows. DMG(P-L) could rise when conflicting masks are disabled and the Loss term shrinks.

**Key equations (transcribed from the rendered PDF).** Notation: op = original profit, fp = final profit, st/et = execution start/end, oam = observation availability mask, f_ijk = mask-enable flag.
- **Eq. (1), time-discounted profit:** fp_j = op_j · exp(−λ · st_j). In §4, λ = 10⁻⁵. Reviewer check: with st in seconds from the horizon start, exp(−10⁻⁵ × 5400) ≈ 0.947, matching the stated "about 95%" at the end of the 1.5 h horizon. The paper does not state the time unit or origin.
- **Eq. (2), objective:** max Σ_i Σ_j Σ_k x_ijk · fp_j.
- **Eqs. (3)–(4), storage and energy:** Σ_j Σ_k x_ijk · sto_j ≤ sto_i^sat, and Σ_j Σ_k x_ijk · ene_j ≤ ene_i^sat, for every satellite i.
- **Eq. (5), consensus:** Σ_i Σ_k x_ijk ≤ 1 for every task j.
- **Eq. (6), inside mask:** st^oam_ijk ≤ st_j < et_j ≤ et^oam_ijk, if x_ijk = 1.
- **Eq. (7), manoeuvre gap:** et_j + amt_jj' ≤ st_j', if et_j < st_j'.
- **Eq. (8), duration:** st_j + dur_j = et_j, if task j is assigned (Σ_i Σ_k x_ijk = 1).
- **Eq. (9):** x_ijk ∈ {0,1}.
- **Eq. (10):** h_ij = 𝟙(DMG_ij > y_ij).
- **Eq. (11), DMG(P):** DMG_ij = max(fp_j) = max(op_j · exp(−λ · st_j)), for j ∉ b_i.
- **Eqs. (12)–(13):** k^DMG_ij = argmin_k st^oam_ijk over masks with f_ijk = 1; st^DMG_ij = st^oam at that mask. The task starts at the start of its earliest enabled mask.
- **Eq. (14):** j^add = argmax_j (DMG_ij · h_ij), for j ∉ b_i with h_ij = 1.
- **Eqs. (15)–(18):** B_i = B_i + 1; b_iB_i = j^add; z_ij^add = i; y_ij^add = DMG_ij^add.
- **Eqs. (19)–(20), timestamps:** s_ii' = t (the receive time from sender i'); s_ii'' = max{s_ii'', s_i'i''} for every other i'' ∉ {i, i'}.
- **Eqs. (21)–(24), release (n̄ = bundle position of the first task with z_ij ≠ i):** y_i,b_in = 0 and z_i,b_in = −1 for n **>** n̄; b_in = ∅ for n **≥** n̄; B_i = n̄ − 1. The first released task keeps the y/z values received from the neighbour, as in CBBA; tasks after it are reset.
- **Eq. (25), DMG(P/S):** DMG_ij = max(fp_j / sto_j), for j ∉ b_i.
- **Eq. (26), DMG(P-L):** DMG_ij = max(fp_j − l_j), for j ∉ b_i. l_j is "the expected profit sum of those masks which conflict with the final assigned mask in the temporary constraints" (§4). How "expected profit" is computed is not stated.

**Table 1 (transcribed exactly).** Action rule for sat_i (receiver) on communication with sat_i' (sender) about task_j. Default action: leave. i'' ∉ {i, i'}; i''' ∉ {i, i', i''}.

| Sender thinks z_ij is | Receiver thinks z_ij is | Action of sat_i (default: leave) |
|---|---|---|
| i' | i | if y_i'j > y_ij → update |
| i' | i' | update |
| i' | i'' | if s_i'i'' > s_ii'' or y_i'j > y_ij → update |
| i' | none | update |
| i | i | leave |
| i | i' | reset |
| i | i'' | if s_i'i'' > s_ii'' → reset |
| i | none | leave |
| i'' | i | if s_i'i'' > s_ii'' and y_i'j > y_ij → update |
| i'' | i' | if s_i'i'' > s_ii'' → update; else → reset |
| i'' | i'' | s_i'i'' > s_ii'' → update |
| i'' | i''' | if s_i'i'' > s_ii'' and s_i'i''' > s_ii''' → update; if s_i'i'' > s_ii'' and y_i'j > y_ij → update; if s_i'i''' > s_ii''' and s_ii'' > s_i'i'' → reset |
| none | i | leave |
| none | i' | reset |
| none | i'' | if s_i'i'' > s_ii'' → update |
| none | none | leave |

Actions (as printed under Table 1): **update** y_ij = y_i'j, z_ij = z_i'j; **reset** y_ij = 0, z_ij = −1; **leave** y_ij, z_ij unchanged. Note: the sender-i'' block has rows only for receiver i, i', i'' and i'''; there is no "none" row there. A reimplementation must define that case, for example by checking Choi et al. (2009), which this review has not checked. No tie-breaking rule for equal bids is printed.

**Algorithms 1–3 (checked visually).**
- Algorithm 2 computes the bid only as DMG(P) (op · exp(−λ · st^DMG)), starting from DMG_ij = 0 and st^DMG = the planning horizon. There is no pseudocode for DMG(P/S) or DMG(P-L).
- Algorithm 3 runs the action rule for every task, then the timestamp update. Release and mask recovery run only if conf_i = 0.
- In Algorithm 3's release loop, the iteration that finds n̄ also passes the "n̄ ≠ −1" test, so it resets y and z at position n̄ as well. This contradicts Eqs. (21)–(22), which reset only n > n̄. The same iteration also empties b_in before indexing y and z by it.

**Mask-flag assignment: settled, the pseudocode is inverted.** The rendered pages show:
- Definition (§3.1): "f_ijk = 1 if oam_ijk is available, f_ijk = 0 otherwise."
- Prose (§3.2 step 4): "If any constraint is not satisfied, change the corresponding mask enable flag f_ijk = 0."
- Algorithm 2: "if oam_ijk conflicts with task_jadd then f_ijk = 1 else f_ijk = 0".
- Algorithm 3: "if oam_ijk conflicts with any task in b_i then f_ijk = 1 else f_ijk = 0".

Both algorithms assign the flag the opposite way to the definition and the prose, and Algorithm 2 only selects masks with f_ijk = 1. So the pseudocode is inverted, most likely a typo. Even with the values swapped, Algorithm 2's else branch would re-enable masks that an earlier bundle task had disabled. A consistent reading is:
- Algorithm 2: set f_ijk = 0 when the mask conflicts with the newly added task and leave it unchanged otherwise.
- Algorithm 3: recompute every flag against the bundle that remains, as 0 if it conflicts with any task and 1 otherwise.

**baselines:**
- **What was run:** the paper compares only its own bid variants, DMG(P) against DMG(P/S) (Table 2) and DMG(P) against DMG(P-L) (Table 3).
- **What was not run:** there is no centralised optimiser, no plain CBBA without the constraint check, and no CNP or other auction run.
- **CNP comparison:** the claim of lower communication than CNP rests only on an analytical bound: the communication count is "significantly lower than N·M" (§4, observation 1).
- **Optimality:** the comparison with centralised performance is cited, not shown: "CBBA is equivalent to the centralized sequential greedy algorithm" [24] (§4, observation 2).

**metrics:** Available target number (targets with at least one window), "communication number" (the exact definition is not stated in the text), completion number, completion rate (%), total profit (Tables 2–4). Also the number of repeated claims per iteration and the number of iterations to global consensus (Figs. 3–4, §4). Wall-clock or on-board runtime is not reported.

**scale:**
- 30, 60 or 90 satellites in 3 planes; 500, 1000 or 1500 targets, global (60°S–60°N) or regional (3°N–53°N, 73°E–133°E).
- 1.5 h horizon (04:00–05:30, 30 July 2022). Profit and storage drawn uniformly from [50, 100]. Maximum storage 750, about 10 tasks per satellite.
- The time discount parameter is λ = 10⁻⁵, which gives about 95% of the original profit at the end of the horizon (§4).
- Communication counts range from 3,241 to 55,574 in Tables 2–3, and reach 58,520 in Table 4 (regional, 1500 + 100 tasks). Convergence took 56 iterations (global-1500-90) and 68 (regional-1500-90), and 58 and 71 after the dynamic arrival (§4).
- **Figures (read visually, approximate):**
  - Fig. 1: an STK 3D view and a 2D ground-track map of Walker-δ 30/3/1 at t = 0. It shows no numeric results.
  - Fig. 2: the 30 satellites are drawn on a circle, coloured by plane (1–10 blue, 11–20 green, 21–30 red). Straight lines mark the ISLs available at t = 0.
  - Fig. 3a (global-1500-90): repeated claims fall from about 6,600 at iteration 1 to about 2,000 by iteration 8. There is a plateau near 1,000–1,200 over iterations 18–26, then a decline to about 0 by about iteration 50. The x-axis ends at 56.
  - Fig. 3b (regional-1500-90): a smooth decay from about 8,800 at iteration 1 to near 0 at about iteration 68. The x-axis ends at 69.
  - Fig. 4a: the same shape as Fig. 3a up to about 600 at iteration 29, then a jump to about 1,600 at iteration 30. It returns to near 0 by about iteration 50. The x-axis ends at 58.
  - Fig. 4b: about 1,550 at iteration 29, then a jump to about 3,300 at iteration 30, decaying to near 0 by about iteration 71. The x-axis ends at 72.
- Implemented in C++ on an Intel i9-11900K desktop. No runtime figures (§4).

**key_limitations:**
- **No external baselines** and no optimality gap.
- **Idealised network:** no latency, loss or bandwidth model. Inter-plane links use a simple line-of-sight rule. Clocks are synchronised with the ground. The ground precomputes link windows.
- **No termination detection:** the planner simply runs to a time limit.
- **No faults, health or safety model.**
- **Energy not exercised:** it is in the formulation but no values are given.
- **Single run per configuration:** no seeds or variance are reported.
- **Claims stronger than the evidence:**
  - The paper says ICBBA can "improve the completion rate and the total profit" under dynamic arrival. Table 4 shows profit rising but completion rate falling (92.32→87.53% global, 89.12→88.76% regional).
  - DMG(P/S) beats DMG(P) in only "five out of nine" global instances (§4).
- **Convergence of the bid variants:** the CBBA convergence and greedy-equivalence guarantees are not re-established for the coupled-constraint DMG variants.

**AstraMesh_overlap:**
- **Where it fits:** this is the closest direct template for a satellite-adapted CBBA (B3). Bundle construction checks feasibility per observation window against storage, energy and slew timing. After outbid releases, previously disabled windows are recovered. Messages carry only bids, winners and timestamps over a time-varying ISL graph (§3).
- **Gaps relative to AstraMesh:**
  - AstraMesh has 4 CubeSats, versus 30–90 here. The paper's assumption of always-on intra-plane rings likely does not hold for AstraMesh.
  - AstraMesh involves crosslink constraints, health beliefs, faults and protection modes, none of which the paper models.
  - AstraMesh targets GNSS TEC observations rather than optical imaging.
- **Use with care:** any reuse should add explicit message delay and loss, and a termination or freeze rule. It should also check that the bid function used is diminishing, since the paper does not show this.

## 3. replication_priority

**high.** It is the most directly applicable satellite-specific CBBA variant. The paper gives full pseudocode for the main loop, bundle construction and consensus check (Algorithms 1–3), and points to public instances.

On reimplementation as B3: the algorithm is described in enough detail to rebuild as a satellite-adapted CBBA baseline.
- **Now known from the rendered pages:** the discount formula (Eq. 1, fp = op · exp(−λ · st), λ = 10⁻⁵), the bid rule (Eqs. 11–14) and the timestamp and release rules (Eqs. 19–24). Table 1 is transcribed above. The paper takes it from Choi et al. 2009, but as printed it has no sender-i''/receiver-none row and no tie-break rule, so those should be checked against Choi et al. 2009.
- **Still to be chosen and documented by AstraMesh:**
  - the energy model;
  - the agile execution-time heuristics;
  - the time unit and origin for st;
  - how the "expected profit" in the Loss term of DMG(P-L) is computed.
- **Pseudocode not to follow literally:**
  - The mask-flag assignment in Algorithms 2–3 is inverted relative to the definition and prose (confirmed above). Use the consistent reading given there.
  - Algorithm 3's release loop also resets y and z at position n̄, which contradicts Eqs. (21)–(22). Follow the equations.
