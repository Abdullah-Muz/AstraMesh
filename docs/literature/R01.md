## 2. Analysis columns

**problem:** a measurement study of how 2024 solar storms affected Starlink network performance and orbits, resolved by region, orbit and satellite (§1).

**agent_info / method:** none; no algorithm is proposed. Data: 76 RIPE Atlas probes on Starlink (AS 14593), pinging DNS root servers and seven anchors every 4 min (§3.1, Fig. 1); Space-Track TLEs (about every 10 h) propagated with SGP4; altitude change between consecutive TLEs as the drag proxy (§3.3). Four storms: 11 May (Dst −406 nT), 12 Aug (−188 nT), 8 Oct (−148 nT), 11 Oct (−333 nT) (Fig. 2).

**networking_assumptions:** observed, not modelled. RTT normalised by the minimum over a 7-day window (§3.2).

**physics:** atmospheric drag only, inferred from TLE altitude changes; no density model. About 5 800 Starlink satellites, 4 708 analysed after removing orbit-raising ones (§3.3).

**fault_model / safety_model:** none.

**findings (as reported):**
- Regional peak RTTs shift in time across regions; the authors attribute this partly to orbital motion, and say "this interpretation is speculative" (§3.2, Fig. 3).
- Altitude change varies strongly between orbital planes of the same inclination, in a "W" pattern with plane orientation relative to the Sun; most and least affected planes are about 90° apart (§3.3, Figs. 4, 5, 9; quiet-period comparison Fig. 10).
- The most affected satellites (above the 95th percentile, 0.325 km) are at high latitudes, over the South Atlantic Anomaly, and on the day side at peak Dst (§3.4, Figs. 6, 7).
- Only 30 of 236 most affected satellites on 11 May were also among the most affected on 12 May; neighbouring satellites were affected next, which the authors attribute to Starlink's automatic orbit maintenance (§3.4, Fig. 8; §4).

**proposals (discussion only):** "storm-aware autonomous control modes that temporarily modify or disable certain self-driving behaviors", "collective decision-making algorithms that consider space weather conditions", and "probabilistic impact models" (§4).

**key_limitations:** TLE gaps of about 10 h; 4-min ping resolution; probes only between 25°N and 55°N; Starlink is opaque (no hop information); altitude changes mix storm decay with operator manoeuvres; the SAA group mixes a radiation-exposure explanation with a drag measurement (§3.4: "enhanced exposure due to a weakened Earth's magnetic field").

**AstraMesh_overlap:**
- Supports the premise of G3 with real data: storm impact on a constellation is heterogeneous across satellites and changes from day to day, so identical-risk replays (Phase 4 S11) are the unrealistic case.
- It is evidence for the **drag** channel (increment 2), not for radiation. AstraMesh keeps the channels separate (spec §6.3); R01's SAA category should not be cited as drag evidence.
- Names the gap (storm-aware modes, collective decisions) without proposing or testing a method. Not a competing method.
