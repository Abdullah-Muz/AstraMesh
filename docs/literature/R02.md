## 2. Analysis columns

**problem:** choose a launch constraint on GOES integral proton flux that avoids launching into an SEP event that exceeds the hardware design environment.

**method:** every GOES data point from 1986–2017 is a test launch time (3 365 281 of them). A launch is "no-go" if the flux exceeded the launch constraint in the 15 min before launch; the mission is violated if the flux exceeded the design environment during the mission. P(false go) = false negatives / total cases; P(false no-go) = false positives / total cases. Parameters: constraint and design environment 10–1 000 pfu, mission length 15 min – 1 day, > 10, > 50 and > 100 MeV channels.

**findings (as printed):** P(false go) is roughly proportional to mission length; P(false no-go) falls as an inverse power law of mission length. A higher constraint with a lower design environment raises false go and lowers false no-go. Compared qualitatively with ACE/SIS heavy ions, the > 10 MeV protons "correlated better with the heavy ion fluxes than the > 50 MeV protons did".

**key_limitations:** probabilities are over all cases, not conditional on an event; persistence of the last 15 min is the only "forecast"; the heavy-ion comparison is qualitative; poster only.

**AstraMesh_overlap:**
- The closest framing found for H5's "at a given safety-risk budget": a protection decision traded as false go (missed hazard) against false no-go (lost operations). It is single-asset and reactive, with no forecast model and no distributed decision.
- Weak support for using the ≥ 10 MeV channel as the radiation proxy (Phase 4, DL-068).
