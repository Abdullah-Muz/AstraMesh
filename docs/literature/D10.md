## 2. Analysis columns

**problem:** flight results of four technologies on four 6U CubeSats: crosslink MANET, onboard distributed decision-making (DSA), optical navigation (StarFOX), and autonomous manoeuvre planning (ROMEO) (abstract).

**agent_info:** DSA runs three cFS apps per spacecraft (Comm, TEC, Autonomy). The Autonomy app uses a MILP solver to allocate GPS channels from local TEC rewards and peers' information (p.7, Fig. 7). "Consensus exists when all of the spacecraft have the same plan" (p.7).

**networking_assumptions:** B.A.T.M.A.N. mesh over CesiumAstro SDR crosslinks; network reliability "typically much greater than 90%"; spacecraft were usually all in direct range, so routing tests switched off a radio module; files over 50 kB were unreliable (pp.4–5).

**physics:** near-SSO at about 575 km; in-train formation at 50–100 km, then passive safety ellipses; formation manoeuvres were commanded from the ground (pp.2–3, 12).

**fault_model:** not a subject of the paper. Starling-1 had a propulsion leak and a radio uplink limitation, so it never joined the DSA network: the DSA demonstrations used 3 spacecraft (pp.3, 7).

**safety_model:** ROMEO manoeuvres were screened on the ground for conjunctions with a final ground go/no-go; limits of 0.6 m/s per plan and 0.292 m/s per manoeuvre. ROMEO never executed a manoeuvre (navigation-filter frame error, command timing) (p.5).

**method / results (DSA):** GPS channel selection for TEC observation, "shared sampling" and "simultaneous sampling" (p.6). In one 1.5 h window, consensus held in 893 of 2 709 one-second ticks; mean time to re-consensus 2.04 s, worst 46 s (p.8, Figs. 9–10). The authors claim "the first fully distributed autonomous operation of multiple spacecraft" (p.8).

**baselines / metrics:** the ground compared DSA's channel allocations with an optimal partition (p.6); no quantitative comparison is printed here (it refers to the 4S and SmallSat 2024 DSA papers).

**scale:** 3–4 spacecraft, months of operation.

**key_limitations:** no space-weather response, protection modes or health beliefs are described; DSA's science decisions were not used operationally (each spacecraft downlinked its full GPS data, p.6).

**AstraMesh_overlap:**
- Confirms the prior art in Finding 1: four-CubeSat, crosslinked, distributed onboard planning of ionospheric TEC observations has flown. AstraMesh cannot claim that.
- Nothing here covers decentralized protection-mode coordination under space weather (G3) or health beliefs under communication loss (G1). Starling 1.5 plans "send and forget" autonomy and turning crosslink radios on and off for science (p.11), not protection.
