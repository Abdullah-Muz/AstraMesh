## 2. Analysis columns

**problem:** how valuable is a forecast of CME arrival time, alone, for deciding when to take mitigating action, and how much do CME speed and magnetic field strength add (§1).

**method:** OMNI hourly solar wind, 1995–2019; a coupling function G as the geoeffectiveness measure (Eq. 1); action is needed when G exceeds its 99.5th percentile G′ (climatological p = 0.005). A catalogue of 512 interplanetary CMEs gives a hypothetical perfect arrival-time forecast. **Cost-loss analysis** (Murphy 1977): action costs C, an unmitigated event costs L; the forecast's potential economic value is V = 100 × (T_F − T_CL) / (T_CL − T_P), where T_F, T_CL and T_P are the total costs with the forecast, with climatology and with a perfect forecast (Eq. 2) (§2).

**results:** G′ is exceeded with probability 0.0442 in CME hours and 0.0015 otherwise, so a perfect arrival-time forecast adds value only for 0.0015 < C/L < 0.0442, i.e. only where false alarms are cheap (§2, Fig. 1, peak V about 64 %). Adding even coarse CME speed (quartiles) or field intensity extends the useful C/L range; both together extend it most (Fig. 1). The paper names "placing a communications satellite in safe mode" as a case where C is a substantial fraction of L, i.e. high C/L (§2).

**key_limitations (stated):** hourly point-by-point comparison ("double penalties" from timing errors); one action threshold; no time history of storms; similar results for 24 h windows are reported but not shown (§3).

**AstraMesh_overlap:**
- Gives a standard, citable way to value a protection policy when the loss from an unprotected hazard is not known: report value across the whole C/L range instead of fixing one ratio. AstraMesh's payload tolerance and loss are synthetic (Phase 4, DL-068), so a C/L sweep would show for which operating assumptions each policy (P-A / P-B / P-C, and the G3 candidate) is worth using.
- Its warning matches the Phase 4 finding: forecasts that raise false alarms are worth it only when protection is cheap relative to the loss.
- Single-asset, non-distributed; geomagnetic rather than proton hazard. Not a competing method.
