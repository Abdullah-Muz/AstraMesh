## 2. Analysis columns

**problem:** measure how far the geomagnetic cutoff for ≳ 80 MeV protons moved during the SEP events and storm of 13–15 December 2006, orbit by orbit (abstract, §1).

**data and method:** PAMELA on Resurs-DK1 (70° inclination, 350–610 km, 3-axis stabilised), protons 12–18 Dec 2006 (§2.1). Cutoff latitude in AACGM coordinates per rigidity bin, 13 logarithmic bins over 0.39–3.29 GV, taken as the latitude where the flux falls to half its mean above 65°, fitted over single ~94 min orbits; cross-checked by back-tracing protons through IGRF-11 plus TS05 (and T96 for comparison) driven by 5-min OMNI solar wind (§2.2–2.4).

**results:** the cutoff moved equatorward during the storm main phase, by about **6° at ~80 MeV (0.39–0.46 GV)** and about 2° at ~3 GeV; TS05 matched the measurements better than T96 (T96 underestimated by up to 4 %, TS05 overestimated by up to 2 % in the main phase); the cutoff variations correlate with Kp, Dst and SYM-H (0.80, 0.78, 0.78), Kp better in the initial phase, Dst and SYM-H in the main and recovery phases (§4, Figs. 2–4). Data gap 13 Dec 10:00 – 14 Dec 09:14 UT (system reset).

**key_limitations:** one storm; rigidities from 0.39 GV up, i.e. protons of ~80 MeV and above, not ≥ 10 MeV; cutoff estimates averaged over whole orbits.

**AstraMesh_overlap (increment 3):**
- Supports a per-spacecraft radiation risk built from a cutoff latitude that moves equatorward with storm intensity, driven by SYM-H, which the S11 series already contains (DL-069).
- A ~6° shift is large compared with how far the four spacecraft are apart (20 km), so storm-time suppression alone will not make their risks differ much; differences come mainly from where each spacecraft is in its orbit when the flux arrives. Increment 3 must quantify this (DL-078 states the hypothesis cannot be tested if risk is nearly identical).
- Its numbers are for ≳ 80 MeV; using them for the ≥ 10 MeV hazard channel would be an extrapolation and must be labelled as such.
