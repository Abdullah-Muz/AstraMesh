## 2. Analysis columns

**problem:** flight results of DSA, collaborative allocation of GPS channels for multi-point ionospheric TEC observation across the Starling swarm, demonstrating "fast, autonomous reactions to relevant scientific data" (abstract).

**agent_info:** three cFS apps per spacecraft: COMM (messages over the ad hoc network, RTI Connext DDS Micro), TEC (relative TEC from dual-band GPS; exploit reward ∝ TEC, explore reward = distance to the GPS satellite), AUTO (plan) (pp. 3–4, Fig. 2). Each AUTO instance gathers all spacecraft's rewards and solves the same MILP, so that every spacecraft "has the same information, and can solve the same problem, and obtain the same answer" (p. 4).

**networking_assumptions:** the Starling MANET; consensus is lost whenever rewards or visibility sets differ between spacecraft, e.g. from network delay (pp. 6, 10).

**physics:** none modelled in the paper; GPS visibility comes from flight data.

**fault_model:** none. One spacecraft (Starling-1) never joined, so DSA ran with 3 spacecraft; the paper reports this as meeting the minimum, not full, success criterion (Table 1).

**safety_model:** none described. Non-consensus ticks can produce "invalid" plans, e.g. assigning a GPS satellite not in view, corrected on the next tick (p. 6).

**method:** MILP (Fig. 3, Eqs. 1–4): maximise a blend of explore, exploit and coverage rewards subject to per-spacecraft channel capacity, solved with lp_solve at 1 Hz and regenerated each tick (pp. 4–5).

**baselines:** none in flight; the Key Performance Parameters compare against a state of the art, a threshold and a goal (Table 2).

**metrics and results:**
- DSA ran in 16 of 28 experiment periods (57.14 %); 13 of the 16 were successful or loosely successful, which the text gives as "approximately 86.7%" after platform stability. 13/16 is 81.25 %, so the paper's figure does not follow from the counts given (p. 5, Fig. 4).
- Consensus in 893 of 2 709 ticks of one 1.5 h interval, often holding only a few seconds; full coverage in 689 of those 893 (p. 6).
- Reconfiguration time (KPP 165): mean 2.04–6.44 s per experiment period, worst case 46 s, against a 22 min threshold and a 5 min goal (p. 10, Figs. 9–10).
- Uplink reduction (KPP 164): 0.493 by compressed table size, 0.613 by command count, with 3 of 4 spacecraft; Table 2 gives a 66 % threshold and 75 % goal, so both measured values are below the threshold. The "≈ 70 %" that "meets the threshold" is an expectation for 4 spacecraft, not a measurement (pp. 8–9).

**scale:** 3 spacecraft, 28 experiment periods of 24–48 h.

**key_limitations:** no faults, health beliefs, protection modes or space-weather response; consensus requires identical information on every spacecraft; results come from short intervals; no comparison with other planners.

**AstraMesh_overlap:**
- Settles Finding 1 from the full text: distributed onboard allocation of GPS-TEC observations across a crosslinked CubeSat swarm has flown (with 3 spacecraft). AstraMesh must not claim it.
- DSA's "everyone solves the same problem" consensus is the opposite of what G3 needs: protection must stay local and act without agreement (spec §6.4; R04). DSA's frequent loss of consensus under small differences supports that.
- Nothing here addresses G1–G4 directly.
